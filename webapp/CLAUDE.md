@AGENTS.md

## Pages

- `/` — Musical Box: ring connection, session recording, song generation. Each person has a fixed ring, passed to `RingCard` as `ringName`: Person 1 wears size 9 `R02_AF03`, Person 2 size 11 `R02_D7B0`. Its Connect button names that ring and Chrome's chooser lists only it ("Choose a different ring" lists any). Chrome remembers a chosen ring across reloads only with `chrome://flags/#enable-web-bluetooth-new-permissions-backend` and `#enable-experimental-web-platform-features`; then Connect finds it by name without the chooser.
- `/syncwave` — full-screen heartbeat waves for a TV, opened in a tab by the SyncWave button on `/`: one wave per ring mirrored around a shared axis, each a range of ridges with solid flanks, both always travelling right, no figures (`components/waveform.tsx`). The Athens Voice key visual (`public/athens-voice.jpg`) fills the screen behind them (`object-cover`, a shade darker: a 16:10 screen loses a sliver of each side, never a gap) and the waves are drawn in light over it (Waveform `glow`: their flanks veil the picture, `GLOW_FLANK`, instead of hiding it; otherwise the waves are as they were without the picture); when the song plays a "Your song is ready!" label sits on the axis and the waves move with the song instead of the hearts: `/` works out the song's loudness in the bass and the melody from its file (`lib/song-levels.ts`, downloaded once the song is playing so it does not hold up its start) and, while it plays, sends the levels of the moment about 30 times a second (`music` messages); the red wave swells with the bass, the blue with the melody, and each kick flashes the axis. New Session hands the waves back to the hearts. It has no colour-mode switch of its own: the waves are always drawn in light over the picture, and the mode set on `/` only tints its labels and the bar. Old `/syncwave/v1`–`v3` links redirect here (`next.config.ts`). It has no rings of its own: `/` broadcasts each ring's latest heart rate over a `BroadcastChannel` (`lib/heart-rate-channel.ts`), so both pages must be open in the same browser. Red (Person 1) and blue (Person 2) turn pink only within about 1 BPM of each other (`PINK_GAP`), keeping a trace of their own colour; equal BPMs flash on the axis and tug the waves together every 2.4 s. The broadcast also carries the session's wait (`plan`: start time and expected ms, from `WINDOW_SEC`/`FIRST_MEASUREMENT_SEC`/`SONG_EXPECTED_SEC` in `app/page.tsx`, set again when the song is asked for: at both rings' first measurement, or `MUSIC_BY_SEC` at the latest), shown as a bar under the waves on the `placebo()` curve in `lib/progress.ts` (a steady pace to about 67%, then a smooth slowdown: about 82% at the expected time, still creeping towards 99%; the music bar on `/` uses it too), and `song`: set when the song starts playing on `/` (the player's `onStarted`: at once if the browser will not play it, or `SONG_START_GRACE_MS` 20 s after it is shown if the sound is still loading), which brings the label and hands the waves to the song's `music` levels as they come, until New Session. Quiet waiting music (`public/waiting-music.mp3`, a Suno take; `components/waiting-music.tsx`) plays on `/syncwave` from the first click on that tab (the browser allows sound only then; until then it says "Click anywhere to turn on the music"), carries on through Start, fades out the moment the song starts on `/`, starts again from the top with each New Session (`round` in the broadcast) and fades out and in again at the end of the track. Its volume is set by the slider under Now Playing on `/` (`musicVolume` in the broadcast, kept in localStorage via `lib/waiting-music.ts`). `?mock` replays a recorded session from the database, on the live site too; `?mock=<session id>` picks one; add `&song` to see the bar and the song arrive 15 s in, the waves then dancing to a stand-in beat.
